VNest V2 · Living Frames · media reality check · 13 Oct 2026

Curated media shortlist

Three clips and eight photographs proposed as visual references for one controlled local test of the Living Frames composition. Nothing here has been downloaded into the repository or wired into the prototype: every preview on this page is loaded straight from Pexels' own image server, so this page needs a network connection.

None of this is VNest. Every asset is third-party stock, shot in other buildings and other cities (one in Bangkok, one in London, several in Hà Nội and Hồ Chí Minh City, per the creators' own descriptions). In any prototype it must keep the existing “MINH HOẠ · không phải hình ảnh VNest” marking, and it must be replaced by commissioned VNest photography before anything is shown as a real property.

Video clips

V1PRIMARY

Cozy Modern Bedroom with Minimalist Decor

Creator
Cá Bảo
Source
pexels.com/video/…-39952442
Format
16:9 · 3840×2160 · 9 s · 30 fps (1080p and 1440p files offered)
Placement
Frame 01, the showcase. Native 16:9 on desktop; the 3:2 phone crop keeps 84% of the width.
Why
A slow lateral move from the balcony door across the bed: daylight, greenery outside, one calm subject. It reads as “a room you could wake up in”, which is what the booking action sells.
Watch for
A camera move does not loop cleanly: play once and hold, or cross-fade the loop. A massage chair enters at the right edge near the end; trim to roughly the first 6 s.

V3ALT. PRIMARY

Đường sá Hà Nội buổi tối

Creator
Bao Linh
Source
pexels.com/video/…-20079154
Format
16:9 · 3840×2160 · 42 s · 24 fps
Placement
Frame 05, “Khu phố”, or the showcase in the atmosphere-led comparison below.
Why
A locked-off evening view down a tree-lined avenue; the traffic supplies the motion, so it loops without a visible jump. 24 fps gives it the most filmic cadence of the three.

Reserve: Luxurious Modern Bedroom with Natural Light by Alef Morais (16:9, 3840×2160, 8 s, 30 fps). A stronger push-in than V1, but a teal cast and a resort pool outside the window, which fights the warm paper palette and the urban story.
